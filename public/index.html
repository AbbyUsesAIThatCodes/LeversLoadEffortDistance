Quick Help: hover or focus a dotted term or ? button to read its explanation. Click or tap to keep it open; click or tap again, tap elsewhere, or press Escape to dismiss it.

  1. Use Controls to change either mass or its distance from the fulcrum. Drag the gold Load, teal Effort, purple Fulcrum, or their labels along the beam.
  2. Hold Level and Hide Math let you predict. Release to see which side goes down, then reveal the calculation. While released, the beam keeps responding as you move objects; grabbing alone does not level it. When the turning effects match, the weighted pointer gently returns the beam to level. Watch for Settling… followed by Balanced.
  3. Swap Positions exchanges exact beam coordinates. Each object keeps its own mass, color, shape, and role. The fulcrum stays still. Swap twice to return.

Controls: the gold Load and teal Effort panels follow their objects’ beam positions when swapped. Orbiting the camera does not exchange panels. On a narrow screen, the controls share a bottom dock; close it to return to the math. Arm lengths are measured from the fulcrum, with a minimum of 75 mm. Masses and distances use 25 g and 25 mm steps. Negative fulcrum coordinates are toward the beam’s marked left end. The fulcrum stays between the objects.

Keyboard: Tab to a role label, then use left/right arrows to move across the screen. Up/down changes Load or Effort mass. Sliders, number boxes, and ½/2× buttons are also available. Drag the background to orbit; scroll or pinch to zoom. Side View and Fit View restore useful views.

Forces and Motion: the gold Load rests directly on the beam and tilts with it; the teal hanging Effort pulls through its cord. Both applied forces point down, even when an object moves up. These descriptions fit this apparatus, not every possible lever.

Balance Pointer: the brass-and-purple pointer is fixed to the beam and rotates with it. Its 250 g bob sits 125 mm below the axle when level. Gravity pulls it back toward its alignment mark. It moves with the fulcrum and contributes no turning effect at level. Small differences between the load and effort can settle at a small tilt.

Ideal Model: the Load, Effort, and pointer bob have mass. The beam, cord, pointer rod, and other attachments are massless; the pivot is ideal. The crate stays fixed at its selected beam position without sliding or tipping independently. It represents a downward point load at the labeled beam-axis point. The model does not simulate the full crate’s center of mass or the extra torque its height would produce when tilted. Both load and effort forces act vertically through their labeled points; arrows show force direction.

Distance and Motion: arms run along the beam from the fulcrum to each force point. When tilted, both horizontal moment arms shrink by cos(angle). The pointer adds a restoring turning effect when tilted, while the equation for level balance stays the same. The math panel shows turning effects at level. Motion uses an illustrative point-mass inertia and damping, limited to ±12°; it is not a calibrated acceleration simulation.

Mechanical Advantage: IMA is effort-arm length divided by load-arm length. Load force divided by effort force equals IMA only at ideal balance. A longer effort arm needs less effort force to balance the same load. The two masses experience the same gravity, so comparing mass × arm length gives the same balance prediction.
